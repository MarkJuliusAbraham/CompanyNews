# Capital Markets: Intermediate

How instruments are valued, issued, traded, and settled.

## Equity Markets

- [ ] **Initial Public Offerings (IPOs)**: The process of a private company first selling shares to the public.
- [ ] **Follow-On Offerings**: Additional share sales by a company that is already public.
- [ ] **Underwriting and Bookbuilding**: How banks gauge investor demand, set a price, and take on issuance risk.
- [ ] **Short Selling**: Selling borrowed shares in the hope of buying them back at a lower price.
- [ ] **Margin Trading**: Using money borrowed from a broker to increase the size of a position.
- [ ] **Corporate Actions**: Events such as stock splits, buybacks, and dividends that affect a company's shares.
- [ ] **Equity Valuation Ratios**: Measures such as P/E and EV/EBITDA used to compare company values.
- [ ] **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)**: Valuing an asset as the present value of its expected future cash flows.

## Fixed Income

- [ ] **Bond Pricing**: Calculating a bond's price by discounting its coupons and principal.
- [ ] **Yield to Maturity**: The total annualized return if a bond is held until it matures.
- [ ] **Yield Curve**: A plot of yields across maturities, often read as a signal of economic expectations.
- [ ] **Duration and Convexity**: Measures of how sensitive a bond's price is to interest rate changes.
- [ ] **Credit Spreads**: The extra yield investors demand over government debt for taking on credit risk.
- [ ] **Investment Grade vs. High Yield**: The divide between higher-rated bonds and riskier "junk" bonds.
- [ ] **Repurchase Agreements (Repos)**: Short-term loans backed by securities, sold now and bought back later.
- [ ] **Securitization**: Pooling loans and selling securities backed by their cash flows.
- [ ] **Mortgage-Backed Securities**: Bonds backed by pools of home or commercial mortgages.

## Derivatives

- [ ] **Forwards**: Private agreements to buy or sell an asset at a set price on a future date.
- [ ] **Futures**: Standardized, exchange-traded versions of forward contracts.
- [ ] **Options**: Contracts giving the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell at a set price.
- [ ] **Interest Rate Swaps**: Agreements to exchange fixed interest payments for floating ones.
- [ ] **Hedging vs. Speculation**: Using derivatives to reduce an existing risk versus to take on a new one.
- [ ] **Margin and Mark-to-Market**: Collateral posted against positions that are revalued daily.

## Market Structure

- [ ] **Order Books**: The live record of buy and sell orders at each price.
- [ ] **Market Makers**: Firms that quote both buy and sell prices to provide liquidity.
- [ ] **Clearing and Settlement**: The steps that confirm a trade and transfer cash and securities.
- [ ] **Central Counterparties (CCPs)**: Clearinghouses that stand between buyers and sellers to absorb default risk.
- [ ] **Custodians**: Institutions that hold and safeguard securities for investors.
- [ ] **Dark Pools**: Private trading venues that do not display orders before execution.

## Portfolio and Risk

- [ ] **Modern Portfolio Theory**: Building portfolios that maximize expected return for a given level of risk.
- [ ] **Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM)**: A model linking an asset's expected return to its market risk.
- [ ] **Alpha and Beta**: Return above a benchmark, and sensitivity to overall market moves.
- [ ] **Sharpe Ratio**: Return earned per unit of risk taken.
- [ ] **Types of Risk**: The main categories, including market, credit, liquidity, and operational risk.
- [ ] **Value at Risk (VaR)**: An estimate of the potential loss on a portfolio at a given confidence level.
- [ ] **Active vs. Passive Investing**: Trying to beat the market versus simply tracking it.

## Macro and Regulation

- [ ] **Monetary Policy**: How central banks use rates and other tools to manage inflation and growth.
- [ ] **Economic Indicators**: Data such as GDP, CPI, and employment figures that move markets.
- [ ] **Securities Regulation**: Rules on disclosure, investor protection, and market conduct.
- [ ] **Insider Trading and Market Abuse**: Illegal practices that exploit private information or distort prices.
