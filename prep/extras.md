# Capital Markets: Extras

Adjacent topics, history, and practical context.

## Alternative Assets

- [ ] **Private Equity**: Investing in private companies or taking public companies private.
- [ ] **Venture Capital**: Funding early-stage companies in exchange for equity.
- [ ] **Hedge Funds**: Lightly regulated pooled funds that use a wide range of strategies.
- [ ] **Private Credit**: Lending to companies directly by non-bank investors.
- [ ] **Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs)**: Tradable companies that own or finance income-producing property.
- [ ] **Convertible Bonds**: Bonds that can be exchanged for a set number of the issuer's shares.
- [ ] **Special Purpose Acquisition Companies (SPACs)**: Listed shell companies formed to take a private company public.

## Digital Assets and Technology

- [ ] **Cryptocurrencies**: Digital assets such as Bitcoin that are recorded on decentralized ledgers.
- [ ] **Stablecoins**: Crypto tokens designed to hold a steady value, usually against the US dollar.
- [ ] **Tokenization**: Representing ownership of real-world assets as tokens on a blockchain.
- [ ] **Decentralized Finance (DeFi)**: Financial services run through smart contracts rather than intermediaries.
- [ ] **FIX Protocol and Market Data**: The messaging standard and data feeds behind electronic trading.
- [ ] **AI in Finance**: Using machine learning for research, trading, and risk management.

## History and Case Studies

- [ ] **Bretton Woods System**: The postwar currency order that tied exchange rates to the dollar and gold.
- [ ] **1929 Crash and the Great Depression**: The collapse that led to modern US securities regulation.
- [ ] **Black Monday (1987)**: The largest one-day percentage drop in US stock market history.
- [ ] **Long-Term Capital Management (1998)**: The near-collapse of a highly leveraged hedge fund that required a rescue.
- [ ] **Dot-Com Bubble**: The late-1990s surge and crash in internet stocks.
- [ ] **2008 Global Financial Crisis**: A crisis triggered by US mortgage losses that spread through global banks.
- [ ] **Flash Crash (2010)**: A sudden, minutes-long plunge and recovery in US stock prices.
- [ ] **GameStop Short Squeeze (2021)**: A retail-driven rally that put pressure on short sellers and brokers.

## Behavior and Thinking

- [ ] **Efficient Market Hypothesis**: The theory that prices already reflect available information.
- [ ] **Behavioral Finance**: How psychology and cognitive biases influence investor decisions.
- [ ] **Bubbles and Manias**: Episodes where prices detach from fundamentals, driven by crowd behavior.
- [ ] **Reflexivity**: The idea that market prices and fundamentals influence each other.

## Practical Skills

- [ ] **Reading Financial Statements**: Interpreting the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement.
- [ ] **Reading SEC Filings**: Finding key information in reports such as the 10-K, 10-Q, and S-1.
- [ ] **Python for Finance**: Using tools such as pandas and NumPy to analyze market data.
- [ ] **Bloomberg Terminal and Market Data Tools**: The professional platforms used for prices, news, and analytics.

## Broader Context

- [ ] **ESG and Sustainable Investing**: Considering environmental, social, and governance factors in investment decisions.
- [ ] **Emerging Markets**: Investing in developing economies, with higher growth potential and added risks.
- [ ] **Sovereign Debt Crises**: What happens when governments struggle to repay what they owe.
- [ ] **Global Regulatory Bodies**: Authorities and standard-setters such as the SEC, ESMA, and FCA.
- [ ] **Careers in Capital Markets**: Roles across the buy side, sell side, and market infrastructure.
