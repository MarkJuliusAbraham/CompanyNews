# Capital Markets: Foundational

The core concepts, instruments, and participants.

## Market Basics

- [ ] **Capital Markets**: Markets where governments and companies raise long-term funding from investors.
- [ ] **Primary vs. Secondary Markets**: New securities are sold by issuers in the primary market, then traded between investors in the secondary market.
- [ ] **Money Markets**: Markets for short-term borrowing and lending, typically under one year.
- [ ] **Exchanges vs. Over-the-Counter (OTC)**: Trading on a centralized, regulated venue versus directly between two parties.

## Core Instruments

- [ ] **Stocks (Equities)**: Shares representing partial ownership of a company.
- [ ] **Bonds**: Loans to a government or company that pay interest and return the principal at maturity.
- [ ] **Treasury Bills**: Short-term government debt sold at a discount to face value.
- [ ] **Commercial Paper**: Short-term, usually unsecured debt issued by large companies.
- [ ] **Mutual Funds**: Pooled investment vehicles priced once a day, at the close of trading.
- [ ] **Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs)**: Pooled funds that trade on an exchange throughout the day, like a stock.
- [ ] **Commodities**: Physical goods such as oil, gold, and wheat that trade on specialized markets.
- [ ] **Foreign Exchange (FX)**: The market for trading one currency against another.
- [ ] **Derivatives (Overview)**: Contracts whose value depends on an underlying asset, rate, or index.

## Market Participants

- [ ] **Issuers**: Governments and companies that sell securities to raise money.
- [ ] **Retail vs. Institutional Investors**: Individuals investing their own money versus organizations such as pension funds and insurers.
- [ ] **Investment Banks**: Firms that help issuers raise capital and advise on deals.
- [ ] **Broker-Dealers**: Firms that execute trades for clients or trade on their own account.
- [ ] **Asset Managers**: Firms that invest money on behalf of clients.
- [ ] **Regulators**: Authorities such as the SEC that set and enforce market rules.
- [ ] **Central Banks**: Institutions that set monetary policy and influence interest rates.
- [ ] **Credit Rating Agencies**: Firms that assess how likely a borrower is to repay its debt.

## Key Concepts

- [ ] **Time Value of Money**: Money today is worth more than the same amount in the future.
- [ ] **Interest Rates**: The cost of borrowing money and the return for lending it.
- [ ] **Risk and Return**: Investors generally demand higher potential returns for taking on more risk.
- [ ] **Diversification**: Spreading investments to reduce the impact of any single loss.
- [ ] **Liquidity**: How easily an asset can be traded without moving its price much.
- [ ] **Bid, Ask, and Spread**: The best prices to sell and buy at, and the gap between them.
- [ ] **Market Capitalization**: A company's share price multiplied by its shares outstanding.
- [ ] **Dividends**: Portions of company profit paid out to shareholders.
- [ ] **Bond Yield**: The return a bond earns relative to its price.
- [ ] **Market Indices**: Baskets such as the S&P 500 that track a segment of the market.
- [ ] **Order Types**: Instructions such as market and limit orders that control how a trade executes.
- [ ] **Bull and Bear Markets**: Sustained periods of rising and falling prices.
- [ ] **Inflation**: The general rise in prices, which erodes the real value of returns.
