# Capital Markets: Advanced

Pricing theory, structured products, trading strategies, and systemic issues.

## Derivatives Pricing

- [ ] **Black-Scholes Model**: The classic closed-form formula for pricing European options.
- [ ] **Binomial Trees**: Option pricing by stepping through possible up and down price moves.
- [ ] **The Greeks**: Measures such as delta, gamma, and vega that show how an option's price responds to inputs.
- [ ] **Implied Volatility and the Volatility Surface**: The volatility priced into options, mapped across strikes and expiries.
- [ ] **Risk-Neutral Pricing**: Valuing derivatives as discounted expected payoffs under a no-arbitrage probability measure.
- [ ] **Monte Carlo Simulation**: Pricing and risk estimation by simulating many possible market paths.
- [ ] **Exotic Options**: Non-standard contracts such as barrier, Asian, and lookback options.
- [ ] **Stochastic Volatility Models**: Models such as Heston that treat volatility as changing randomly over time.

## Rates and Credit

- [ ] **Short-Rate Models**: Models such as Vasicek and Hull-White that describe how interest rates evolve.
- [ ] **Yield Curve Construction**: Building a consistent rate curve from market prices, including bootstrapping.
- [ ] **Swaptions, Caps, and Floors**: Options on interest rate swaps and on floating rate levels.
- [ ] **SOFR and the LIBOR Transition**: The move from survey-based benchmarks to transaction-based overnight rates.
- [ ] **Credit Default Swaps**: Contracts that transfer the risk of a borrower defaulting.
- [ ] **Collateralized Debt Obligations**: Pools of debt split into tranches with different risk levels.
- [ ] **Credit Risk Models**: Structural and reduced-form approaches to estimating default probability.
- [ ] **Valuation Adjustments (xVA)**: Pricing corrections for counterparty credit, funding, and capital costs.

## Trading and Market Microstructure

- [ ] **Price Discovery**: How trading activity incorporates new information into prices.
- [ ] **Market Impact and Transaction Costs**: How the act of trading itself moves prices and erodes returns.
- [ ] **Algorithmic Execution**: Strategies such as VWAP and TWAP that split large orders to reduce impact.
- [ ] **High-Frequency Trading**: Automated, very low-latency trading that profits from small price differences.
- [ ] **Smart Order Routing**: Automatically sending orders to the venues offering the best execution.
- [ ] **Limit Order Book Dynamics**: How order flow and queue position shape short-term prices.

## Portfolio and Quantitative Strategies

- [ ] **Multi-Factor Models**: Explaining returns through factors such as value, momentum, and size.
- [ ] **Statistical Arbitrage**: Trading price relationships between related assets using quantitative models.
- [ ] **Risk Parity**: Allocating a portfolio so each asset class contributes equally to risk.
- [ ] **Black-Litterman Model**: Combining market equilibrium returns with an investor's own views.
- [ ] **Long/Short and Market-Neutral Strategies**: Pairing long and short positions to isolate a specific source of return.
- [ ] **Basis and Relative Value Trades**: Exploiting pricing gaps between closely related instruments.
- [ ] **Backtesting and Overfitting**: Testing strategies on historical data while avoiding results that are just noise.

## Risk and Systemic Issues

- [ ] **Expected Shortfall**: The average loss in the worst scenarios beyond the VaR threshold.
- [ ] **Stress Testing**: Estimating portfolio losses under severe hypothetical or historical scenarios.
- [ ] **Counterparty Risk**: The risk that the other party to a contract fails to pay.
- [ ] **Liquidity Risk and Funding Spirals**: How falling prices and tighter funding can reinforce each other.
- [ ] **Basel III and Capital Requirements**: Global rules on how much capital and liquidity banks must hold.
- [ ] **Dodd-Frank and MiFID II**: Major post-crisis reforms in US and EU financial markets.
- [ ] **Systemic Risk and Contagion**: How one institution's failure can spread through the financial system.
